
# Analysis Results

## 1. Research Objective

This study examines the statistical relationship between
economic growth and selected dimensions of quality of life
across the 27 European Union Member States over the period
2010–2024.

The analysis is observational and comparative. The
country-year is the primary unit of observation.

The main research question is whether higher economic
growth is associated with improvements in quality of life
and whether these relationships differ across its dimensions.

## 2. Data and Variables

The study uses annual data obtained from Eurostat.

The main indicators include:

- Real GDP per capita growth (annual percentage change).
- Median equivalised disposable income.
- Employment rate among people aged 20–64.
- At-risk-of-poverty or social exclusion rate (AROPE).
- Life expectancy at birth.

A composite quality-of-life index was also constructed
using four dimensions: median income in PPS, employment,
AROPE and life expectancy.

The composite index covers 2015–2024 and 26 countries.
Ireland was excluded from the index because of income
data comparability issues.

## 3. Methods

The following statistical methods were applied:

- Descriptive statistics.
- Pearson correlation.
- Spearman rank correlation.
- Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) regression.
- Panel regression models with country and year fixed effects.
- Country-clustered standard errors.
- Leave-one-country-out sensitivity analysis.
- Annual standardisation of indicators for the composite index.

All results are interpreted as statistical associations,
not as evidence of causality.

## 4. Cross-Country Analysis

The cross-country analysis compares average economic
growth with changes in selected quality-of-life indicators.

| Quality-of-life dimension | Countries | Pearson r | OLS coefficient | R-squared |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Real median income growth | 26 | 0.866 | 1.584 | 0.749 |
| Employment change | 27 | 0.810 | 3.051 | 0.656 |
| AROPE change | 27 | -0.638 | -1.916 | 0.408 |
| Life expectancy change | 27 | 0.489 | 0.281 | 0.239 |

All four OLS relationships were statistically significant
at the 5% level.

The strongest associations were observed for real income
growth and employment improvement.

The negative coefficient for AROPE indicates that
countries with higher average GDP growth tended to record
larger reductions in the at-risk-of-poverty or social
exclusion rate.

The relationship with life expectancy was positive but
weaker than the relationships observed for income and
employment.

## 5. Composite Quality-of-Life Index

The composite index combines four standardised indicators:

1. Median equivalised income measured in PPS.
2. Employment rate.
3. Reversed AROPE rate.
4. Life expectancy at birth.

Each component has an equal weight.

The indicators are standardised separately for each year.
Consequently, the index measures the relative position
of a country compared with the other countries in the
sample, rather than absolute living standards.

The correlation between average GDP growth in 2015–2024
and the average composite index was negative:

- Pearson correlation: -0.611 (p = 0.0009).
- Spearman correlation: -0.644 (p = 0.0004).

The leave-one-country-out analysis confirmed that the
negative Pearson relationship remained stable. Its value
ranged from approximately -0.711 to -0.566.

This result should not be interpreted as evidence that
economic growth reduces quality of life. It reflects
differences in the relative positions of countries
within the sample.

## 6. Panel Regression Results

Panel models included country and year fixed effects.
Standard errors were clustered by country.

The following table presents joint models containing
both current and one-year-lagged GDP growth.

| Dependent variable | Observations | Current GDP coefficient | Current p-value | Lagged GDP coefficient | Lagged p-value | R-squared |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Employment change | 378 | 0.1374 | 0.006 | 0.1106 | <0.001 | 0.593 |
| Life expectancy change | 378 | 0.0162 | 0.051 | -0.0246 | 0.046 | 0.472 |
| Composite index relative change | 234 | 0.0041 | 0.114 | 0.0075 | 0.019 | 0.239 |

### Employment

Both current and lagged GDP growth were positively
associated with annual employment changes.

The results suggest that the relationship between
economic growth and employment may extend beyond
the current year.

### Life Expectancy

The estimated current GDP coefficient was positive
but narrowly missed the 5% significance threshold.
The lagged coefficient was negative and statistically
significant at the 5% level.

These estimates are borderline and should be treated
with caution. They do not demonstrate that economic
growth reduces life expectancy.

### Composite Quality-of-Life Index

Current GDP growth was not statistically significant.
One-year-lagged GDP growth showed a positive association
with the annual change in the composite index.

Because the index measures relative country positions,
this result concerns changes in relative position,
not absolute improvements in quality of life.

## 7. Hypothesis Assessment

- H1: Economic growth is associated with real income growth.
  Supported by the results.

- H2: Economic growth is associated with improvements
  in employment. Supported by the results.

- H3: Economic growth is associated with reductions
  in AROPE. Supported by the results.

- H4: Economic growth is associated with improvements
  in life expectancy. Partially supported; panel estimates
  are mixed.

- H5: Economic growth is associated with improvements
  in the composite quality-of-life index. Results are mixed
  and depend on the type of analysis.

## 8. Limitations

Several limitations should be considered:

1. The observational design does not allow causal conclusions.
2. The composite index includes 26 countries rather than 27.
3. AROPE data are analysed for 2015–2024.
4. Changes in AROPE methodology may affect comparability.
5. Equal weights in the composite index are a simplifying choice.
6. Annual standardisation makes the index relative rather
   than absolute.
7. HICP deflation is an approximation of changes in
   household purchasing power.
8. Panel models use only 26–27 country clusters, so
   statistical inference requires caution.
9. Irish GDP growth is unusually affected by multinational
   enterprises and international asset movements.
10. The COVID-19 pandemic and the subsequent inflation
    period may have influenced the results.

## 9. Conclusions

The results indicate that economic growth in EU countries
is positively associated with several important dimensions
of quality of life, particularly real income growth and
employment improvement.

Higher economic growth is also associated with larger
reductions in the AROPE indicator.

The relationship with life expectancy is weaker and
less consistent across the different models.

The composite index analysis produces mixed results,
highlighting the importance of distinguishing between
cross-country differences and changes within individual
countries over time.

Overall, GDP growth alone is not sufficient to describe
changes in quality of life. A broader assessment should
also consider income distribution, employment, poverty
and population health.

All findings should be interpreted as statistical
associations rather than causal effects.
